# EVERROOT — "SAY SOMETHING"
### Hero Intro Film · Director's Shooting Script + Treatment

**Format:** 1 film, ~1:50. Cuts down to :60, :30, and a 6-part social series (one character each).
**Aspect:** 2.39:1 hero cut · 9:16 vertical alt · **Language:** blended — English + native tongues, subtitled in warm serif.
**Palette:** the EverRoot golden-dusk. Grain. Real light. No gloss.

---

## DIRECTOR'S STATEMENT (the strategy)

Everyone has felt it, so I refuse to *explain* it. I'm going to make the audience *feel the specific ache*: not death in the abstract, but the small, unbearable thing — **forgetting the exact sound of someone's voice.** The way they said your name. The laugh. The pause before advice.

The film is built as a **relay**. No single character carries it. A grandmother in Lagos begins a sentence; a widower in Oslo finishes it; a girl in Oaxaca answers it. Loss is the one language we all already speak — so the edit treats a dozen strangers as **one grieving, hoping human voice.** That's the emotional engine, and it's also the product truth: EverRoot is a *chorus of voices kept alive.*

We earn the tree. We do not open on the tree. We open on the wound. Then EverRoot arrives not as an app — as **the answer to a fear.**

---

## CAST — the chorus (deliberately global; loss is universal)

- **ODESSA**, 78, Black American, New Orleans — makes gumbo from memory, never written down.
- **DANIEL**, 34, Korean American, Los Angeles — lost his mother eight months ago.
- **CAMILA**, 11, Mexican, Oaxaca — her abuelo's stories.
- **TOM**, 71, white, rural Yorkshire — a widower, alone at a kitchen table for two.
- **AMARA**, 29, Nigerian, Lagos — pregnant; her father won't meet the baby.
- **YUSUF**, 62, Lebanese, Beirut — recording proverbs for grandchildren abroad.
- **PRIYA**, 40, Indian, Mumbai — playing an old voicemail she's terrified to delete.
- **NILAK**, 8, Inuit, Nunavut — learning a song from a great-grandmother on a screen.

They never meet. The **edit** is what introduces them to each other — and to us.

## THE SCRIPT

### BEAT 0 — BLACK (0:00–0:07)
Darkness. One held breath. The soft hiss of a real room-tone recording.

> **OLD MAN (V.O., unseen — YUSUF):** *(gently, close to the mic)* "Go on. Say something. Anything. It doesn't have to be perfect."

A tiny red **REC** dot ignites in the black. Hold. Then —

**SUPER (fades up, serif, warm):** *The first thing you forget…*

---

### BEAT 1 — THE ORDINARY LIGHT (0:07–0:28)
*A montage of un-filmed life. Handheld, warm, imperfect — the footage nobody thought to keep.*

- ODESSA's hands folding roux into a pot; steam; she hums, no words.
- TOM and a WOMAN (we barely see her) laughing at something off-frame, tea going cold.
- CAMILA on ABUELO's knee, his mouth moving, her eyes huge.
- AMARA dancing barefoot with her FATHER at a wedding, both mid-laugh.
- NILAK's GREAT-GRANDMOTHER singing, cracked and beautiful.

The relay begins — each voice hands the sentence to the next:

> **ODESSA (V.O.):** "…is the sound —"
> **DANIEL (V.O.):** "— of their voice."
> **PRIYA (V.O.):** "Then the laugh."
> **TOM (V.O.):** "Then the way they said your name."

**SUPER:** *…is the sound of their voice.*

Music enters — a single piano note, then a lone cello. No drums yet.

---

### BEAT 2 — THE ABSENCE (0:28–0:50)
*The exact same rooms. Emptier. The light hasn't changed — the people have.*

- ODESSA's pot, but now she stands over it frowning: *what did Mama put in it?*
- TOM sets **two** mugs down. Catches himself. Removes one. The scrape of the second mug is the loudest sound in the film.
- DANIEL scrolls his phone in the dark, thumb hovering over a contact he can't call.
- AMARA, pregnant, alone in a doorway, one hand on the frame where the dancing was.

> **DANIEL (V.O.):** "Eight months. I called just to hear the voicemail. Last week…"
> *(beat)*
> **DANIEL:** "…the number was reassigned. A stranger answered."

Silence. Let it hurt. Room-tone only.

> **CAMILA (V.O.):** *(small)* "I can remember his stories. I can't remember his voice telling them."

**SUPER:** *We don't fear the ending. We fear the fading.*

---

### BEAT 3 — THE CONFESSION (0:50–1:05)
*Faces, now. Close. Direct address — each admitting the same private thing, as one voice.*

> **PRIYA:** "I keep the voicemail because —"
> **AMARA:** "— I'm scared that if I let it go —"
> **TOM:** "— there'll be no one left who sounds like home."
> **ODESSA:** "I keep meaning to ask her how she made it."
> **CAMILA:** "And now there's no one left to ask."

The cello finds a second voice. A held, aching chord.

---

### BEAT 4 — THE TURN (1:05–1:22)
*The hinge. A hand reaches for a phone — but this time to RECORD, not to mourn.*

YUSUF, unhurried, sits a young grandchild beside him and taps record. ODESSA, defiant, pulls a stool to the stove and narrates as she cooks. AMARA presses record and rests the phone on her belly.

> **YUSUF:** *(to the future, not to us)* "This is for you. For the one I might not get to meet."
> **ODESSA:** "First — you burn nothing. You *listen* to it."
> **AMARA:** *(a breaking smile)* "Hi, baby. This is your grandfather laughing. I want you to know exactly what he sounded like."

The REC dot from Beat 0 returns — and this time it **blooms into gold.**

**SUPER:** *So keep it. All of it.*

---

### BEAT 5 — EVERROOT IGNITES (1:22–1:38)
*Now — and only now — the product. We pull back from a single glowing recording into the LIVING LEGACY FOREST.*

Each recorded voice becomes a point of light that travels up into the dark canopy. The tree's **veins ignite** — memory running through it like a pulse. **Lanterns** kindle along the branches, one per part of a life: *Life Advice. Recipes. Traditions. The Stories. Messages for the Future.* The forest fills with the warm light of many trees — a whole community of the remembered.

Music opens up — the two voices become a small choir. First warmth of percussion, like a heartbeat.

> **NARRATOR (V.O., single warm voice):** "EverRoot. Every voice you love — rooted, and growing."

---

### BEAT 6 — REUNION ACROSS TIME (1:38–1:48)
*The payoff. Years later. Someone who came after taps a lantern — and the person is there again.*

A CHILD we haven't met — unmistakably AMARA's, a few years old — presses a glowing lantern. From it, warm and whole:

> **AMARA'S FATHER (V.O.):** *(laughing, the same wedding laugh)* "There she is. There's my girl."

The child laughs at a joke from a man who died before she was born. Cut to DANIEL — eyes closed, his mother's voice filling the room again, no stranger this time. Cut to CAMILA, older now, mouthing along to Abuelo's story in his voice.

Hold on the tree, fully lit, generations of light in its branches.

---

### BEAT 7 — CLOSE (1:48–1:52)
Everything settles to the single gold point of light we started with, resting in the dark.

**LOGO:** EVERROOT

**TAGLINE (primary):** *The people we love don't have to fade.*

Final sound: a real recorded voice, half a second, mid-laugh — then gently, the REC dot goes dark. But the light in the tree stays.

## SOUND & MUSIC
- **Score:** solo piano → cello → two strings → small human choir → single heartbeat drum in Beat 5. Never triumphant. **Tender, not epic.**
- **The hero instrument is silence.** Beats 2 and 3 must have the courage to be quiet. The scrape of Tom's second mug and the room-tone after "a stranger answered" are the two loudest moments in the film.
- **Real recordings only** for the V.O. laughs — cast for *texture*, not polish. A slightly cracked voice beats a perfect one every time.

## DIRECTION NOTES
- **No actor performs grief at the camera.** They perform *love* and *memory*; the grief is the audience's job. We shoot warmth; the empty chair does the crying.
- **Ethnic range is structural, not decorative** — the relay edit only works because the sentence crosses borders. Cast real families where possible; use their real kitchens, their real songs.
- **The tree is a reveal, not a set.** Withhold it until Beat 5. If we open on the product, we've lost.
- **Golden-dusk unbroken** from first frame to last, so the ache and the hope share one light — grief and remembrance aren't two worlds, they're the same room at a different hour.

## HOW IT CUTS DOWN
- **:60** — Beats 0, 2 (Tom's mug + Daniel), 4 (Amara), 5, 7.
- **:30** — Daniel's "a stranger answered" → Amara records → tree ignites → tagline.
- **6-part series** — each character's full arc as a standalone short, ending on the same logo, so the campaign itself becomes a chorus.
